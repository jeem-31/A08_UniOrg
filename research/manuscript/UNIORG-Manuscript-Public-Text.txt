CHAPTER I
INTRODUCTION
1.1 Project Context


Efficient organizational governance and streamlined document processing are essential pillars of a well-functioning university student affairs system. At Laguna State Polytechnic University, administrators and student leaders have observed increasing challenges in managing organization activities, document accreditation, and student feedback. These issues are heavily influenced by the reliance on manual paper transactions and scattered online platforms, which lead to delayed approvals, disorganized records, and a lack of secure communication spaces for students. To understand the depth of this concern, the researchers conducted preliminary interviews with key university personnel and faculty members, namely OSAS Chairperson Christian P. San Luis, LPT, PhD, OSAS Secretary Nicole D. Bartolome, Organization Senior Adviser Rachiel R. Rivano, PhD, and Organization Junior Adviser Harlene G. Origines. The feedback gathered revealed a common observation: while the student organizations are highly active, the administrative burden of manually tracking accreditation documents, managing event records, and gathering honest student feedback requires a modernized, centralized digital intervention to sustain operational efficiency and improve student services.
In many Philippine universities, student affairs offices face the same shift toward digital record keeping, and several institutions already use centralized portals for organization transactions, which shows that manual paper based processing is no longer enough for a campus the size of LSPU.
The accreditation requirements show how heavy the manual work is. Each organization submits a letter of application for renewal and recognition, updated constitution and by-laws, renewal form, program of activities for one year, adviser commitment form, certification of officers, and list of officers and members with one by one picture. At the organization level, officers handle events, payments and elections through scattered online apps, students have no single place for information, and many hesitate to give feedback because there is no secure and private channel.
In response, the researchers proposed the development of UniOrg A Configurable University-Wide Student Organization Management Platform with OCR and NLP for Document Processing and Auto-Tagging. This system includes features such as a multi-org architecture for custom organization profiling, an accreditation document repository, an activity master calendar, and centralized payment and election management. To significantly reduce the administrative workload, UniOrg integrates document technologies like Optical Character Recognition (OCR) and Natural Language Processing (NLP) for automated text extraction, auto-tagging, and summarization of submitted requirements. By transforming tedious manual tasks into automated digital workflows, the proposed platform aimed to create a more organized, responsive, and data-driven governance environment.
The technology gap is the absence of a single university wide platform that combines accreditation tracking with OCR and NLP document processing, since existing tools in the campus handle only isolated tasks and cannot read, tag and summarize accreditation papers automatically. This need is supported by the gap analysis in Chapter II, where existing systems lack the combined accreditation and AI document features summarized in Table 2.1.
The platform is strictly accessible through a web-based application, ensuring a standardized and centralized experience for all users. User interactions are governed by strict role-based access OSAS administrators can monitor university-wide compliance and verify documents, organization officers can manage day-to-day operations and students can view announcements, access academic resources, and securely submit feedback through their respective web portals. The specific development tools and technologies used in this project are PHP (Laravel Framework), Supabase, Tesseract OCR, Cloud Vision API, Cloud Natural Language API, Groq AI models, Cloudflare R2 (S3-compatible storage), HTML, CSS, JavaScript, DomPDF, PHPSpreadsheet, and PDF Parser, and the system is built with scalability, usability, and security in mind.
 Once fully implemented, UniOrg is expected to significantly enhance institutional transparency, streamline document processing, and improve communication, thereby contributing to a highly efficient and modern academic environment.
Beyond convenience, this digital shift matters for the quality of student services. By turning manual tasks into organized digital processes with strict checking and AI assisted tracking, the project supports SDG 4 on Quality Education and SDG 16 on Peace, Justice and Strong Institutions, so that organizations and administrators work with honesty, clear responsibility and readiness.
1.2 Research Objectives
1.2.1 General Objective
The general objective of this study is to design, develop, test and evaluate UniOrg: A Configurable University-Wide Student Organization Management Platform with OCR and NLP for Document Processing and Auto-Tagging, a web based platform for the Office of Student Affairs and Services of LSPU Santa Cruz Campus.
1.2.2 Specific Objectives
This study evaluates UniOrg, a multi-org web platform featuring an integrated master calendar, OCR text extraction, and NLP document processing. Built for LSPU–OSAS, UniOrg automates accreditation reviews, centralizes data management, and streamlines student organization governance.  


Specifically, this study aimed to:


1. To design and develop a configurable web platform (UniOrg) that centralizes student organization accreditation, document storage and activity monitoring for the Office of Student Affairs and Services. The platform provides organization profiling with role based access for OSAS, Organization advisers, Organization officers and students.
2. To test the functionalities of the developed UniOrg system based on the following ten (10) major features:
2.1 Multi-Org Workspace and Organization Profiling
2.2 Accreditation Tracking and Document Repository
2.3 Activity Scheduling and Master Calendar Management
2.4 Centralized Payment
2.5 Student Governance and Election System
2.6 Academic Resource Management
2.7 Communication and Feedback Hub
2.8 Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) and Security Management
2.9 Optical Character Recognition (OCR) for Automated Document Data Extraction
2.10 Natural Language Processing (NLP) for Text Summarization and Auto-Tagging
3. To evaluate the user acceptance of the developed UniOrg system among students, student organization officers and OSAS administrators of Laguna State Polytechnic University Santa Cruz Campus using the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) in terms of:
3.1 Quality Factors
3.2 Perceived Ease of Use
3.3 Perceived Usefulness
3.4 Attitude Toward Using
3.5 Behavioral Intention to Use
3.6 Experience/Actual Use




1.3 Conceptual Framework
1.3.1 Components of the Conceptual Framework
The framework is composed of three linked parts. The Input gathers user data from OSAS, officers and students with accreditation and financial records and current operational inefficiencies with system and technology requirements for OCR and NLP. The Process applies Agile Methodology in building OSAS level and organization level modules and use of OCR and NLP variables for extraction and auto tagging with TAM evaluation. The Output is the functional UniOrg System with a feedback loop that returns evaluation results to the inputs for refinement.
These inputs, processes and outputs were drawn from the synthesis of related literature and related systems in Chapter II, which showed that OCR and NLP features answer the manual processing gaps found in existing systems.
1.3.2 Conceptual Framework Diagram
  

Figure 1.1. Conceptual Framework of UniOrg: A Configurable University-Wide Student Organization Management Platform with OCR and NLP for Document Processing and Auto-Tagging
As illustrated in Figure 1.1, the conceptual framework of this study utilizes the Input-Process-Output (IPO) model to explicitly demonstrate the research flow, which is anchored on the identified problem of manual student organization governance.
The Input phase identifies the current operational inefficiencies of the Office of Student Affairs and Services (OSAS). This phase also encompasses the raw organizational and financial records, user data, and the necessary system and technological requirements, specifically the integration of Optical Character Recognition (OCR) and Natural Language Processing (NLP).
The Process phase details the Agile Methodology utilized to develop UniOrg's core features, which are categorized into OSAS-Level Management and Configurable Organization-Level Modules. Crucially, building upon the synthesis of related literature, this phase incorporates the core research variables (OCR and NLP) to automate document data extraction and auto-tagging. Furthermore, this phase establishes the procedure for rigorous system evaluation using the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM).
Finally, the Output phase yields the fully functional UniOrg System. To ensure continuous improvement, a Feedback loop is integrated, connecting the Output back to the Input. This loop signifies that the evaluation results (based on TAM criteria) and any identified resolved or unresolved process bottlenecks are used to further refine the system's inputs and processes, ensuring the platform remains highly effective in addressing the defined research problems.


1.4 Project Purpose
The purpose of this study is to design, develop, and evaluate UniOrg, an innovative university-wide student organization management platform integrated with Optical Character Recognition (OCR) and Natural Language Processing (NLP). This study aimed to provide a centralized, configurable, and efficient digital hub that addresses the challenges of manual accreditation and document processing at Laguna State Polytechnic University. By automating document reading and summarizing narrative reports, the system intends to improve operational efficiency and transparency across all student organizations. Through the integration of AI-driven features, UniOrg aimed to foster a more organized and modern campus culture, ensuring that organizational governance is both accurate and accessible.
This study sought to improve university-wide engagement by providing a unified platform for tracking activities and maintaining a master calendar to prevent scheduling conflicts. The platform is designed to be highly configurable, allowing each student organization to have a dedicated workspace that aligns with their specific needs while remaining under the supervision of the Office of Student Affairs and Services (OSAS). By rewarding organizational transparency and streamlining the submission of requirements, UniOrg aimed to cultivate a more professional and collaborative environment among student leaders and administrators. Ultimately, the findings of this study contributed to the digital transformation of university governance and provide a replicable framework for other educational institutions.

1.5 Scope and Limitations of the Study
1.5.1 Scope of the Study
This study focuses on the design, development, testing, and evaluation of UniOrg, integrating Optical Character Recognition (OCR) and Natural Language Processing (NLP) to address the manual bottlenecks in student organization governance. The scope of the research is strictly confined to the Laguna State Polytechnic University (LSPU) - Santa Cruz Campus. Data collection and system evaluation involved a specific demographic of respondents: the Office of Student Affairs and Services (OSAS) administrators, the officers and organization advisers of the 39 recognized student organizations, the college Deans, and the enrolled students. Furthermore, the research utilizes the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) to evaluate the respondents' user acceptance of the platform specifically in the context of accreditation, document processing, and activity tracking.
The system covers ten major modules from multi-org workspace and accreditation tracking to OCR and NLP processing, and it runs on a web based environment using PHP Laravel, Supabase, Cloudflare R2, Tesseract OCR, Google Cloud Vision and Natural Language APIs, Groq AI models, DomPDF and PHPSpreadsheet. Development and evaluation were conducted at LSPU Santa Cruz Campus within Academic Year 2025 to 2026.
1.5.2 Limitations of the Study
However, the study presents certain limitations in its research boundaries. First, the investigation is strictly bounded to the administrative processes of student organization management; it explicitly excludes the observation and integration of other OSAS operations, such as student discipline, guidance and counseling, university scholarships, and lost and found services. Second, the study does not cover the evaluation of fully automated financial systems; the research scope for payment tracking is limited to manual routing via personal e-wallet accounts, as it does not include the integration of official payment gateway APIs or the university's central accounting office. Consequently, the study does not investigate the processing of university-wide tuition and miscellaneous fees. Lastly, the findings, user acceptance results, and conclusions formulated in this study are limited only to the LSPU Santa Cruz Campus context and may not be generalizable to other LSPU satellite campuses or different educational institutions.
The study is also limited by internet dependency since UniOrg is web based, by time constraints of the academic calendar, and by availability of complete accreditation records from the organizations.
Processes that are unique to certain organizations and not common to all student organizations are also excluded, since the system covers only the general accreditation, document and activity workflows shared across the 39 organizations.

The local systems such as Nexus, RSU accreditation, and OSAM focus on records and permits but their document review remains manual. Payment tracking and election functions appear in a few systems but they are not integrated with accreditation workflows.
2.2.4 Technology Gap
The technology gap is the absence of automated document processing inside student organization management. Existing systems require officers and staff to read, check, and sort accreditation documents by hand. There is also a lack of integration since events, payments, elections, and documents are handled by separate tools or offices. No reviewed system combines a configurable multi organization hierarchy with OCR extraction and NLP summarization in one platform.
2.2.5 Proposed Response to the Gap
UniOrg responds to these limitations through specific features. The manual document review is addressed by OCR for data extraction and NLP for auto tagging and summaries. The fragmented workflows are addressed by placing accreditation, events, payments, elections, and feedback inside one multi-org workspace. The needs of OSAS are addressed by approval tracking, a master calendar, and centralized reports. Each of these features directly answers a limitation found in the existing systems.


For the manual review problem the system provides OCR that reads scanned accreditation files and converts them into encoded data. This removes the need for officers to retype long details from printed forms. The NLP component then tags each file and generates short summaries so OSAS staff can understand a report without reading every page. For the fragmented setup the platform places accreditation tracking, event scheduling, payment monitoring, elections and feedback inside one workspace. Officers no longer switch between separate tools since records, approvals and schedules stay connected. The master calendar prevents date conflicts while the centralized payment ledger keeps collections transparent. The election module secures voting with verified member access and the feedback hub gives members a direct channel to officers. Each feature was selected to answer a specific limitation so the whole system directly responds to the gaps found in Table 2.1.
2.3 Synthesis
2.3.1 Integration of Findings
The reviewed studies agree that a cloud based multi-org architecture can isolate the data of different groups inside one system. RBAC is recognized as the standard way to secure user permissions. OCR reduces encoding errors while NLP makes long documents easier to classify and summarize. The studies also show that these technologies are usually applied separately and not inside one student organization platform.


2.3.2 Identified Gap
The identified gap is functional, technological, and process in nature. The functional gap is the missing automation for accreditation document review. The technological gap is the absence of OCR and NLP inside existing organization management systems. The process gap is the manual and scattered workflow from submission to approval. There is also a contextual gap since generic platforms are not configured for the OSAS processes of a Philippine state university.


2.3.3 Contribution of the Proposed Project
UniOrg contributes a centralized ecosystem that fills these gaps. It automates the extraction, tagging, and summarization of accreditation documents. It unifies organization management, events, payments, elections, and feedback under OSAS supervision. The project provides a working model that other state universities with similar processes can adopt.

3.1 Materials and Methods
The UniOrg platform was developed using a systematic approach that involved conducting fact-finding interviews with key university stakeholders to identify critical administrative needs and carrying out rigorous development activities supported by extensive resource gathering, by utilizing the Agile methodology the researchers successfully aligned the platform functionalities with the specific accreditation requirements of the Office of Student Affairs and Services while implementing modern technological tools to ensure that document processing and organizational management are streamlined effectively for the university
3.2 Research Design
This study utilizes a descriptive-developmental research design to effectively construct and evaluate the UniOrg platform. This method is specifically structured for projects that require the systematic design, development, and comprehensive evaluation of software systems or technological solutions. The research is fundamentally divided into two complementary phases: the developmental phase and the descriptive phase.
3.2.1 Rationale
Descriptive-developmental design was chosen because it covers both the development of the software and the evaluation of its acceptability in a real-world setting.
The developmental phase focuses on the actual technical creation of the centralized management platform. This involves the design and integration of specific features tailored for the Office of Student Affairs and Services (OSAS) and the thirty-nine (39) student organizations of Laguna State Polytechnic University, particularly the implementation of Optical Character Recognition (OCR) and Natural Language Processing (NLP) for document processing and auto-tagging.
Consequently, the descriptive phase assesses the system's acceptability and functionality among its actual end-users. Through quantitative data gathering, the researchers describe the perceptions of OSAS administrators, college Deans, org advisers, organization officers, and regular students. This evaluation is anchored on the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM), utilizing survey questionnaires to measure five specific constructs: Perceived Usefulness, Perceived Ease of Use, Attitude Toward Using, Behavioral Intention to Use, and Experience. This dual-phase approach ensures the platform is both technologically sound and highly acceptable to the campus community.
3.3 Population of the Study
The population of the study consisted of selected stakeholders from Laguna State Polytechnic University - Santa Cruz Campus (LSPU-SCC), who are the intended end-users and primary evaluators of the proposed UniOrg platform. Since the project is designed specifically to streamline campus-wide organization management, document accreditation, and student engagement, the respondents were categorized into five distinct groups: the OSAS administrators, college Deans/Associate Deans, Org Advisers, student organization officers representing the nine (9) College Departments, and the regular students. 
Locale of the Study: This research was conducted at the Laguna State Polytechnic University (LSPU) – Santa Cruz Campus, where the UniOrg platform is intended for implementation.
The regular students and organization officers represent the primary users of the UniOrg platform as shown in Table 3.2, as they were the ones who actively interacted with the multi-org dashboards, utilize the e-voting modules, and manage organization workflows. Respondents from various recognized student organizations at LSPU-SCC were selected to ensure that feedback accurately reflects the diverse demographic of the campus student body. For the purposes of this evaluation, the researchers employed a mixed sampling design by explicitly selecting 428 specific individuals who are directly relevant to the system’s operational implementation. This strategic distribution ensures that the practical perspectives of the main user groups (students and officers) are prioritized, while also incorporating highly valuable feedback from the Org Advisers, College Deans, and OSAS Administrators, who play critical supervisory and administrative roles in the project’s campus-wide adoption and long-term sustainability.

Respondents were provided with adequate time to hands-on interact with the system's core modules, such as uploading narrative reports for OCR text extraction, testing the NLP auto-tagging, and exploring their respective multi-org dashboards, before completing the questionnaire. The researchers were physically present throughout the demonstration to address any inquiries and provide technical clarifications as needed. Upon the conclusion of the testing activity, the accomplished questionnaires were systematically collected immediately to ensure a high response rate and the completeness of the data required for statistical analysis.


3.6 Sampling Design
A mixed sampling design was used to select the participants for this study. Total enumeration was applied to the College Deans since there are only 9 of them, so all Deans were included as respondents. They oversee student organization activities under their colleges, making their evaluation of the system’s scalability, functionality, and impact particularly critical for the university-wide deployment of UniOrg. For the Office of Student Affairs and Services administrators, total enumeration was employed. Since there are only two staff members in the office, selecting both of them as respondents ensured a complete and accurate understanding of the department requirements.
The study used Slovin’s formula n = N / (1 + N(e)^2), where n is the sample size, N is the population, and e is the margin of error. Slovin was applied per group with a margin of error of 0.07. For organization officers with a population of 768, the sample was 161. For organization advisers with a population of 78, the sample was 56. For regular students with a population of 9,613, the sample was 200. The 2 OSAS administrators and 9 Deans were added through total enumeration. The final sample size was 428 respondents.
Stratified random sampling was utilized for the organization advisers, organization officers, and regular students. Slovin’s formula with a margin of error of 0.07 set each group sample based on its own population. From the 78 organization advisers, a sample of 56 was taken. For the organization officers, 161 were selected from a total of 768. Lastly, 200 regular students were chosen from a population of 9,613. Together with total enumeration of OSAS administrators and Deans, the final sample reached 428 respondents, which allowed the researchers to capture diverse perspectives from different levels of the university.
The population lists came from the registrar for students and from OSAS records for officers and advisers, all for AY 2025 to 2026 active organizations and enrolled students only. The 0.07 margin was set with the adviser to keep the total sample at a manageable 428 while keeping per group computation through Slovin. Inclusion covered active officers, advisers and enrolled students in that school year, while inactive organizations, graduated students and non members were excluded. Random selection was done per stratum by drawing from the group lists until each quota was met.

3.8 Project Design
This section describes how the UniOrg platform was planned and structured before and during development. The design was based on the requirements gathered from interviews with OSAS staff and student organization officers, who described the slow manual accreditation process, scattered files, and delayed approvals. From these findings, the researchers identified the major components, processes, resources, interfaces, and technical considerations needed for the solution. Each part of the design answers a specific manual bottleneck found during the preliminary investigation.
3.8.1 System Requirements
The functional requirements are OCR-based text extraction, NLP auto-tagging of documents and AI summarization. They also include multi-org workspaces with profiles and branding, online accreditation application and renewal with status tracking, event proposals with Dean and OSAS approval plus master calendar sync, centralized payment tracking with GCash receipt upload and verification, secure e-voting with one-time ballots and tally publishing, EduShare folders and e-library search, and a feedback hub with complaints, chatbot, and live chat. Role-based dashboards control what each user type sees. The non-functional requirements are system security, good performance, and responsive UI. Security means strict login controls and role-based access in line with the Data Privacy Act of 2012. Performance means the system stays fast even when hundreds of students use it at the same time. Responsive UI means the pages work on both desktop and mobile browsers.
3.8.2 System Design
The platform uses a multi-tenant use case model where each organization has an isolated workspace under one shared system. The main users are OSAS administrators, Deans, org advisers, organization officers, and regular students, and each has defined interactions with the modules. Accreditation flows from officer submission to OSAS review and approval, while event proposals pass through Dean approval before OSAS approval. Data design uses relational models that connect user profiles, organization records, documents, events, elections, and payment logs. Process flows, the context diagram, data flow diagrams, and the entity-relationship diagram documented in the appendices show how data moves between users and modules. Interface wireframes were prepared first so the layout, navigation, and dashboards matched what each role needed before coding started.
3.8.3 System Architecture: 
The system uses a client-server architecture with four layers. The presentation layer is the responsive web interface that all user roles open in their browsers. The application layer holds the Laravel backend logic, including role-based access control, accreditation routing, and document generation. The data layer keeps structured records in Supabase and heavy files in Cloudflare R2. The external services layer connects Tesseract OCR, Google Cloud Vision, Google Cloud Natural Language, and Groq AI models for text extraction, auto-tagging, and summarization. Requests flow from the interface through access checks to the backend and storage, then back with the needed data. Refer to the system architecture diagram for the visual representation of these components and their interactions.

As depicted in figure 3.1, the development of UniOrg followed the Agile methodology, an iterative approach to software development that organizes work into short, time-boxed cycles called sprints, typically lasting one to four weeks, where each sprint produces a working and testable increment of the system (Binboga & Altin Gumussoy, 2024). Unlike traditional waterfall development, Agile prioritizes continuous stakeholder collaboration, rapid feedback integration, and flexible response to evolving requirements, making it particularly suitable for systems whose full functional scope cannot be entirely defined at the (Tetteh, 2024). This methodology was selected for UniOrg because the system's requirements, spanning a configurable multi-org architecture, strict Role-Based Access Control (RBAC), Optical Character Recognition (OCR) for document extraction, and NLP-driven auto-tagging and summarization, required iterative refinement through actual user feedback rather than rigid pre-planned specification.


Specifically, Agile was applied in this study through the following sprint structure based on UniOrg's project Gantt chart. Sprint 1 (Planning, March–April 2026) covered requirements elicitation through interviews with OSAS administrators, org advisers, and student organization leaders, definition of all functional and non-functional requirements, and production of the System Technical Specification (STS). Sprint 2 (Design, May–July 2026) encompassed the system architecture design, entity-relationship diagram, use case specifications, and UI/UX wireframes for the centralized web platform built with PHP (Laravel) and Supabase. Sprints 3 and 4 (Development, July–October 2026) involved the incremental coding and integration of UniOrg's configurable organization modules and advanced AI features using Tesseract OCR, Groq AI models, Google Cloud Vision API, and Google Cloud Natural Language API, with each sprint delivering testable features reviewed against the STS. Sprint 5 (Testing, October–November 2026) covered functional testing, integration testing, technical evaluation, and user acceptance testing utilizing the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM). Sprint 6 (Deployment, November 2026) finalized system deployment and documentation. At each sprint boundary, outputs were reviewed by the project adviser and technical experts, and feedback was incorporated into the next sprint, ensuring that the final UniOrg platform accurately reflects the real operational needs of the Office of Student Affairs and Services (OSAS) and the thirty-nine (39) student organizations of Laguna State Polytechnic University - Santa Cruz Campus.

Non-functional requirements specified performance metrics, ensuring the system responds efficiently under normal load and maintains strict data security across different user roles. The researchers then created a prioritized product backlog to guide development.
Phase 2 - Designing The designing phase involved creating the system architecture, wireframes, and high-fidelity mockups for the web interfaces. Throughout the development process, the team followed strict coding best practices, including version control using Git, continuous integration, and peer code reviews. This approach ensured that the integration of advanced AI APIs did not compromise the speed or security of the core web platform.

Testing was conducted at multiple levels. The researchers employed a black-box testing methodology, focusing on the platform from the end-user's perspective without analyzing the internal code. Testers provided various inputs, such as uploading blurred accreditation forms or simulating high-traffic digital elections, to verify system accuracy and stability. Performance and load testing were also conducted to ensure that the system could handle simultaneous access by hundreds of students across 39 organizations without crashing.
Phase 5 - User Evaluation The user evaluation phase involved deploying the system to the target population and gathering quantitative and qualitative feedback. Each group was asked to complete specific tasks. For instance, OSAS administrators tested the approval of extracted OCR documents, while students navigated the e-Library and event registration. After interacting with the system, participants completed the structured TAM questionnaire to measure perceived usefulness and ease of use. The evaluation sessions revealed valuable insights, such as minor adjustments needed in the NLP auto-tagging labels and UI enhancements for the mobile-responsive view of the web application.
Phase 6 - Refinement The refinement phase involved making final adjustments based on the TAM evaluation feedback. The researchers prioritized the issues and suggestions that emerged, immediately addressing any critical usability problems before the final deployment.
Modifications were made to optimize the OCR scanning accuracy for specific university document formats, and navigation menus were refined for better user experience. The refinement phase also included comprehensive regression testing to ensure that recent changes did not break existing functionalities within other organization modules. Through the Agile methodology's iterative nature, mistakes were corrected quickly, ensuring that the final UniOrg system successfully addresses the complex organizational and accreditation challenges faced by Laguna State Polytechnic University.

3.10 System Architecture
UniOrg's system architecture defines the overall structure and organization of the platform, including its components, modules, and their interactions. The architecture was designed to ensure that the system meets its functional requirements (automated document text extraction, intelligent auto-tagging, centralized dashboard monitoring, and multi-org organization management) as well as its non-functional requirements (security, strict role-based access control, scalability, and performance).
The system follows a modern client-server architecture centered around a responsive web-based platform that caters to all user roles: OSAS Administrators, College Deans, Organization Officers/Org Advisers, and Regular Students. It utilizes a centralized backend server that handles complex data processing, secure multi-org routing, and AI-powered document analysis. The architecture consists of the following key components:
Presentation Layer: A responsive Web Interface built using HTML, CSS, JavaScript, and Laravel's Blade templating engine, designed to adapt seamlessly across desktop and mobile browsers for all stakeholder dashboards.
Application Layer: The core backend logic built with the PHP (Laravel) framework. This layer enforces strict Role-Based Access Control (RBAC), handles the routing of accreditation documents, and utilizes libraries such as DomPDF, PDF Parser, and PHPSpreadsheet for internal document generation and parsing.
Data Layer: Supabase serves as the primary database for structured data storage, handling user credentials, organization records, and election tallies. Cloudflare R2 is utilized as a highly scalable cloud storage solution for safekeeping heavy multimedia files, event evidence, and uploaded accreditation documents.
External Services: Integration of the Google Cloud Vision API and Tesseract OCR for advanced text extraction from images and scanned documents; alongside the Google Cloud Natural Language API for intelligent auto-tagging, and the automated summarization of organizational narrative reports.
 


3.10.1 Hardware and Software Requirements
The development and deployment setup needs a web server that can run PHP applications with a Laravel backend, a Supabase project for the database, and Cloudflare R2 storage for uploaded files and documents. On the client side, users only need a device with an updated web browser such as Google Chrome, Mozilla Firefox, or Microsoft Edge, since UniOrg runs fully in the browser. The AI features need active API access to Tesseract OCR, Google Cloud Vision and Natural Language APIs, and Groq AI models. DomPDF and PHPSpreadsheet handle document and spreadsheet generation on the server.


3.10.2 System Components and Modules
The system is organized into modules that match the main features. User authentication and role-based access control handles login  and view switching between roles. Account management covers user accounts, the organization directory, appointment of deans and officers, and organizational lifecycle records. Organization workspaces give each student organization its own profile, branding, positions, and permission settings. Accreditation covers online application, renewal, document review, and audit logs for OSAS. Document intelligence handles OCR text extraction, NLP auto-tagging and summarization, the document library, and smart search. Events cover proposals, Dean and OSAS approvals, the master calendar, registrations, and financial reports. Elections cover candidate setup, secure voting, and results tabulation. Payments cover membership and events fee, GCash receipt uploads, verification, digital receipts, and collection reports. EduShare and e-library handle folders, file sharing, previews, and resource search. The feedback hub covers complaints, chat FAQs, announcements, and realtime chat. Each role opens its own dashboard: OSAS, Dean, organization admin, and student.
For clarity, the authoritative stack used in the build is PHP Laravel with Supabase and Cloudflare R2 for data and files. Text extraction uses Tesseract OCR with Google Cloud Vision API while tagging and summarization use Groq gpt-oss-20b cloud API with Google Cloud Natural Language API for entity signals. OCR checking relied on softcopy documents from one organization (SOCCS) plus COR samples for registration extraction. The check was functional through black box cases and no formal character level accuracy was measured which is stated here as a limit. Cloud processing also needs stable internet since Groq and Google APIs run online.

3.10.4 User Interface Design
The user interface is built with Laravel Blade templates using HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. It is responsive, so pages adjust to desktop and mobile screens. Each role sees its own dashboard with only the menus and data allowed by its permissions. OSAS administrators see university-wide monitoring, Deans see college-level approvals, organization officers see their workspace tools, and students see announcements, events, resources, and feedback forms. Navigation stays consistent across pages so users always know where they are.
3.10.5 System Communication and Interaction
Secure RESTful APIs connect the web interface, the Laravel backend, and external services. Each request passes through role-based access checks so users only reach data and actions allowed for their role. File uploads go to Cloudflare R2 while records stay in Supabase. Realtime features such as chat, notifications, and e-library updates use Supabase Realtime and Pusher channels. Document processing calls Tesseract OCR, Google Cloud Vision, Google Cloud Natural Language, and Groq AI models, then returns extracted text, tags, and summaries to the platform.

3.14 Statistical Treatment
This section details the specific statistical techniques utilized in the processing, analysis, and presentation of the gathered survey data. Statistical treatment encompasses the comprehensive range of methods applied in quantitative research to examine empirical data, describe it using quantifiable measures, and compare the results against established theoretical frameworks, which, in this study, is the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM). Statistical analysis is vital for adequately sampling the target population, determining relationships or correlations between different system attributes, and measuring the concrete differences between sets of empirical data based on the study's stated objectives.
The careful selection of appropriate statistical methods is paramount to ensuring that the research findings are valid, reliable, and practically meaningful. For this study, the researchers mainly used descriptive statistical techniques to analyze the quantitative data from the evaluation questionnaires. Cross-tabulation and correlation analysis were added to explore relationships between variables, but no hypothesis testing was done. These techniques were deliberately chosen based on the ordinal nature of the data derived from the Likert scale responses. The analysis directly addresses the study's primary objectives, providing clear and interpretable results regarding the end-users' Perceived Usefulness, Perceived Ease of Use, Attitude Toward Using, Behavioral Intention to Use, and overall Experience with the UniOrg platform.
The formal statistical analysis was conducted utilizing the Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS), a robust and widely used software package for statistical analysis in technical and social science research. SPSS provides a comprehensive suite of statistical tools capable of efficiently processing the TAM evaluation data, making it exceptionally well-suited for this study. Additionally, Microsoft Excel was utilized for the initial data entry, preliminary data screening, and the generation of clear visual representations such as charts and graphs to effectively illustrate the evaluation results of the platform's AI integrations and centralized organizational workflows.
Frequency and Percentage Distribution
The Frequency and Percentage Distribution method was utilized to appropriately organize, analyze, and interpret the survey data gathered from the respondents evaluating the UniOrg platform. This fundamental statistical tool was applied to determine the proportion of responses falling under each specific category, particularly the demographic profiles of the users and their corresponding answers across the Likert scale options. By calculating these distributions, the researchers were able to generate a clear, quantitative overview of the participants' feedback and summarize the evaluation results based on the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM).

Frequency distribution is one of the most basic yet fundamental statistical techniques, providing highly valuable insights into empirical data. By accurately counting how many respondents selected each Likert scale option for every questionnaire item, the researchers could quickly identify emerging patterns regarding user acceptance. For example, if a significant majority of the respondents strongly agreed that the UniOrg platform was easy to navigate (Perceived Ease of Use), this would indicate a high level of user satisfaction with the system's interface design. Conversely, if responses were evenly distributed across all five response options regarding a specific functionality, such as the NLP-powered auto-tagging, this would suggest that the stakeholders had mixed opinions and varied experiences regarding that particular feature.
Percentage distribution is particularly useful for comparing responses across different stakeholder groups or various TAM questionnaire items. For instance, the researchers could compare the percentage of OSAS administrators who found the OCR text extraction highly beneficial (Perceived Usefulness) to the percentage of student organization officers who shared the same sentiment. This comparative analysis identifies whether technological acceptance levels significantly differed between distinct user roles. Similarly, the researchers could compare the percentage of respondents who were satisfied with different platform modules, such as the e-voting system versus the manual payment logging, identifying which features were highly effective and which areas required further technical refinement.
The researchers systematically presented the frequency and percentage distributions through comprehensive tables that displayed, for each questionnaire item, the exact number of respondents who selected a specific response option and the corresponding percentage of the total sample it represented. To enhance data interpretability, these tabular presentations were accompanied by appropriate visual representations, such as bar charts and pie charts, making it significantly easier to quickly grasp the overall pattern of the university stakeholders' responses.
Weighted Mean
The Weighted Mean formula was employed to accurately interpret the degree of user acceptance of the UniOrg platform based on the empirical responses gathered from the OSAS administrators, college Deans, faculty advisers, organization officers, and regular students. Since the TAM-based evaluation utilized a structured five-point Likert scale to measure the core constructs (Perceived Usefulness, Perceived Ease of Use, Attitude Toward Using, Behavioral Intention to Use, and Experience), each response category was assigned a specific numerical weight to reflect its relative importance and to compute the central tendency of the stakeholders' feedback.
Formula:
X̄w = ∑(f × x) / N
 
Where:
 
X̄w = Weighted Mean (average Likert score)
 
f = Frequency of responses for each Likert scale option
 
x = Numerical weight assigned to each option (5 = Strongly Agree, 4 = Agree, 3 = Neutral, 2 = Disagree, 1 = Strongly Disagree)


N = Total number of respondents
 
The weighted mean is a measure of central tendency that takes into account both the frequency of each response and the numerical value assigned to that response. It provides a single number that summarizes the overall level of agreement or satisfaction for a particular questionnaire item. The weighted mean is particularly useful for Likert scale data because it treats the response options as if they were on an interval scale, allowing for mathematical operations that would not be appropriate for purely categorical data.


Standard Deviation Formula
The Standard Deviation formula was used to measure the variability or dispersion of the survey responses gathered from the university stakeholders in evaluating the UniOrg platform. It mathematically indicates how much the individual Likert scale responses differ from the computed weighted mean, providing a clearer picture of the consistency and consensus of the participants' perceptions regarding the system's perceived usefulness, perceived ease of use, and overall experience. A low standard deviation indicates that the users' responses are highly clustered around the mean (showing strong agreement among the stakeholders), while a high standard deviation indicates a wider divergence in their feedback.
SD = s = n − 1∑(xi − X)2
Where:
 
s = Sample Standard Deviation
 
xᵢ = Each individual response value
 
X̄ = Mean of all responses
 
n = Total  number of respondents
 
 
Standard deviation serves as a critical measure of variability that indicates how spread out the survey responses are around the computed weighted mean. A lower standard deviation signifies that responses are closely clustered around the mean, suggesting a high level of consistency and consensus among the respondents. This implies that the university stakeholders share similar perceptions regarding that specific aspect of the platform, whether positive or negative. Conversely, a higher standard deviation indicates greater variability in the responses, revealing diverse opinions about the system's features and overall performance. This might suggest that the system workflows seamlessly for certain user groups but not for others, or that different stakeholders have varying administrative expectations.
The standard deviation is particularly insightful when interpreted in conjunction with the weighted mean. For example, if a specific questionnaire item yields a high mean, indicating overall satisfaction with the NLP auto-tagging feature, but also presents a high standard deviation, this reveals that while the majority of users are highly satisfied, a significant minority holds dissenting views. Such instances warrant further investigation to understand why specific users encountered different experiences. On the other hand, a high mean combined with a low standard deviation indicates a robust consensus that the system performs excellently on that given criterion.
The researchers utilized the standard deviation to identify specific questionnaire items where there was significant disagreement among the respondents. These flagged items were subjected to further analysis, often involving the examination of qualitative feedback or open-ended comments to understand the root causes of the divergent opinions. Analyzing these variances helps determine if different user roles (e.g., OSAS administrators versus regular students) hold systematically different views regarding certain platform functionalities, providing valuable insights on how the system can be improved to better serve the entire campus ecosystem.
Furthermore, the standard deviation was employed to assess the internal reliability of the evaluation instrument itself. Items yielding abnormally high standard deviations might indicate that the question was phrased ambiguously or was confusing to non-technical users, leading respondents to interpret it in varied ways. In such cases, the researchers would note these discrepancies for the refinement of future evaluations.
In addition to these primary statistical techniques, the researchers employed supplementary analyses to extract deeper insights from the quantitative data. Cross-tabulation was utilized to examine relationships between distinct variables, such as whether technological acceptance differed based on respondent characteristics like their designated user role, college department, or prior technological experience. Correlation analysis was also conducted to identify significant relationships between the different Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) constructs, such as determining whether stakeholders who rated the platform highly on Perceived Ease of Use also exhibited a strong Behavioral Intention to continue utilizing the system. These supplementary statistical treatments enabled the researchers to develop a highly nuanced understanding of user perceptions and accurately identify the key factors influencing overall satisfaction with the UniOrg platform.

4.2.1 System Overview
UNIORG is built as a configurable university-wide platform made to modernize how the Office of Student Affairs and Services (OSAS) and the student organizations of Laguna State Polytechnic University Santa Cruz Campus handle accreditation, documents and activities. At its core, the system replaces the manual submission of hard copies and logbook tracking with one web platform. Its main purpose is to connect OSAS and the 39 organizations in a single workspace, so papers are submitted, tagged and approved with less delay.
The platform serves five user groups with pages per role: Students, who register with their COR, join organizations and view events and payments; Organization Officers, who submit accreditation papers, propose events and manage members; Org Advisers, who guide and check their organization records; Deans, who review event proposals; and the OSAS administrators, who monitor compliance and approve accreditation with Role-Based Access Control (RBAC). Major functions cover accreditation tracking, OCR text extraction with NLP auto-tagging and summaries, master calendar, centralized payments with receipt upload, e-voting with live tally, and EduShare with e-library. The flow runs as a loop: an officer uploads a requirement, the system reads and tags it, and OSAS reviews the result for approval or return for compliance.

Figure 4.4. Accreditation pipeline database schema.
This group handles org application and renewal. An apply_orgs record holds the request and its status. Each uploaded requirement becomes a processed_documents row with extracted text, summary, and tags. Each AI attempt is logged in document_processing_attempts. Final copies sit in document_library_entries. Past cycles are kept in apply_org_document_archives. Audit tables record who acted and when.


  

Figure 4.5. Events database schema.
This group handles activities. The events table holds the activity and its approval status from Dean and OSAS. Proposals, registrations, and financial reports point back to the event. Registrations connect to student payments when a fee is required.


  

Figure 4.6. Elections database schema.
This group handles e-voting. One election has many candidates and many votes. Each vote links a user to a candidate. Officer turnover after results is recorded in election_officer_transitions.






  

Figure 4.7. Payments database schema.
This group handles collections. The payments table defines a fee of an organization. Each student_payments row is one student payment with proof, status, and verifier. Event registrations can point to a student payment.
  

Figure 4.8. EduShare, e-library, and documents database schema.
This group handles files and resources. EduShare folders hold items and sharing rules. Library resources are published materials. The documents, shared_documents, and student_documents tables track uploads and student requirements.
  

Figure 4.9. Communications and feedback database schema.
This group handles announcements, complaints, chat, and notifications. Announcements reach many organizations through announcement_organization. Complaints and admin_chats link users and organizations. FAQs, chat logs, and notifications support help and updates.
These tables are interconnected through robust primary and foreign key relationships, ensuring that every transaction such as linking a student payment to a specific organization event or associating an accreditation audit with an OSAS administrator is fully traceable. Real-time data synchronization is achieved through Supabase built-in realtime triggers, allowing database updates to reflect instantly on the OSAS dashboard and organization workspaces accessed through web and mobile browsers without manual refreshing. The comprehensive attribute definitions, data types, and constraints governing these tables are detailed in the data dictionary.

4.2.6 Functional and Non-Functional Requirements
The developed UniOrg platform successfully satisfies all the functional and non-functional requirements established during the system analysis phase. Functional requirements pertaining to core operations such as COR-upload user registration, accreditation and document submission with OCR text extraction and NLP auto-tagging, master calendar scheduling, centralized payments, e-voting, and EduShare and e-library sharing were fully operationalized through the Laravel web workspaces and the mobile-responsive browser interface. Behind the scenes, the application layer leverages Gemini 2.5 Flash for automated summarization and classification with Google Cloud Vision and Natural Language processing alongside Supabas.
 On the non-functional side, the platform stays responsive on desktop and mobile browsers since all pages are mobile-responsive. Access control runs on two layers. The COR upload checks identity at registration, and role-based pages make sure students, officers, deans, and OSAS only open their own modules. Password settings complete the protection for day-to-day use.
For maintainability and growth, all 39 organizations share one Laravel codebase while Supabase handles structured records and Cloudflare R2 keeps the uploaded files. This separation lets OSAS onboard more organizations without new servers. The table 4.42 maps every requirement to its module and figure, so each claim in this section points to visible proof realtime to handle data flow.




In terms of non-functional requirements, the system delivers high performance, reliability, and security. Cross-platform compatibility was achieved for Android mobile browser users and standard web browser clients. Data security and structural integrity are maintained through strict PostgreSQL database constraints and Supabase backend management. Crucially, security parameters such as Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) configurations are isolated exclusively to the System Administrator dashboard to prevent unauthorized alterations by standard organization officers.
To explicitly demonstrate alignment between system design and empirical validation, The table 4.42 outlines the requirements-to-results matrix, detailing how each functional scope was verified through formal Black-Box testing phases yielding a 100% success rate.

The development, testing, and evaluation of the UniOrg system yielded highly favorable results that directly address the core research objectives and the identified operational problems within the Office of Student Affairs and Services. The primary issue motivating this project was the reliance on fragmented, manual, and paper-bound workflows for organization accreditation and document management. The empirical data gathered from both the technical black-box testing and the user evaluation demonstrate that UniOrg successfully mitigates these bottlenecks by introducing an integrated, AI-powered digital infrastructure.
Addressing the first objective of system development, the findings show that the implemented architecture utilizing a Laravel monolith with Supabase and Cloudflare R2 successfully bridges the technological gap identified in Chapter II. Related literature and reviews of existing local systems frequently highlight a lack of cohesive university organization platforms capable of handling document intelligence and realtime coordination simultaneously. By integrating Gemini 2.5 Flash for summarization with Google Cloud Vision OCR and Cloud Natural Language auto-tagging, alongside Supabase realtime for live updates, UniOrg provides an enterprise-grade solution that surpasses legacy spreadsheet and messenger-based coordination. Furthermore, isolating Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) configurations exclusively to the System Administrator addresses the critical security gaps often found in decentralized student applications, ensuring that standard officers cannot compromise system integrity.
In relation to the second objective regarding system functionality, the black-box testing results revealed a 100% success rate across 670 step-level verifications. This result is critically important; in the context of university-wide deployment, system reliability is non-negotiable. The flawless execution of modules such as accreditation tracking, OCR and NLP document processing, event approvals, centralized payments, and secure e-voting proves that the logic performs exactly as intended under simulated real-world conditions. The results suggest that the logic performs as intended under the simulated conditions used in testing. These findings align with recent studies in educational information systems, which argue that minimizing administrative latency and automating document categorization significantly reduces the workload on student affairs offices during peak accreditation periods.
Finally, addressing the third objective regarding user acceptability, the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) evaluation yielded a remarkable overall grand mean of 4.64 with a standard deviation of 0.59. This statistical outcome demonstrates that the intended users, including OSAS personnel, college deans, organization advisers, officers, and regular students, find the system exceptionally useful and easy to navigate. The results strongly support existing TAM literature, which consistently reports that high Perceived Ease of Use and Perceived Usefulness directly drive Behavioral Intention to Use in academic management tools. CHAPTER V
SUMMARY, CONCLUSIONS, AND RECOMMENDATIONS
This chapter presents the summary, conclusions, and recommendations of the study based on the results gathered from the testing and evaluation of the developed UniOrg: A Configurable University-Wide Student Organization Management Platform with OCR and NLP for Document Processing and Auto-Tagging. It includes the analysis and interpretation of the data obtained from system testing and user acceptance evaluation conducted among OSAS administrators, Deans, organization advisers and officers, and students of LSPU Santa Cruz Campus.
5.1 Summary
This study aimed to design, develop, test, and evaluate a system for university-wide organization accreditation, document intelligence, and student-OSAS coordination through OCR processing, NLP auto-tagging, and realtime synchronization. The major findings of the study, aligned with the specific research objectives, are summarized as follows:
● System Development (Objective 1): The study successfully designed and developed UniOrg as a web-based system featuring a Laravel monolith with Supabase and Cloudflare R2, serving role-specific workspaces for OSAS administrators, Deans, organization officers and advisers, and students. The developed core modules include COR-based registration with OTP verification and RBAC, multi-organization workspaces, accreditation and renewal tracking, OCR text extraction, NLP summarization and auto-tagging, event proposals with Dean and OSAS approvals plus a master calendar, centralized GCash payments with receipt verification, secure e-voting with officer transition, EduShare and e-library resources, and a feedback hub with chatbot.
● System Testing and Functionality (Objective 2): The developed system underwent rigorous Black-Box testing across ten (10) distinct test cases covering all major features. The testing resulted in a 100% success rate, with all 670 recorded step-level verifications marked as passed and zero (0) failed results. Testing was run under controlled black-box conditions with ten IT testers working from an end-user view.
● User Acceptance Evaluation (Objective 3): The acceptance was evaluated using the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) by 428 respondents, predominantly comprising Regular Students at 200 (46.7%), along with Organizational Officers, Organization Advisers, College Deans, and OSAS Admins. The evaluation yielded an overall grand mean of 4.64, interpreted as Strongly Agree, across all six constructs: Quality Factors (4.66), Experience (4.66), Perceived Usefulness (4.64), Attitude Toward Using (4.64), Behavioral Intention to Use (4.64), and Perceived Ease of Use (4.62).
5.2 Conclusions
Based on the summary of findings and the specific research objectives of the study, the following conclusions are drawn:
● Resolution of Operational Gaps: The development of UniOrg successfully transitions the Office of Student Affairs and Services of LSPU Santa Cruz Campus from a manual, paper-bound workflow to a structured digital infrastructure. The integration of COR-based registration, OCR extraction with NLP auto-tagging, and master calendar coordination directly resolves previously identified operational gaps in accreditation processing, document management, and event oversight.
● Functional Reliability and Readiness: The system is functionally robust and operationally ready for university-wide deployment. Shows functional compliance across the tested scenarios. The flawless 100% pass rate across 670 step-level Black-Box verifications proves thatsuggests that the application logic, AI processing, realtime database synchronization, and role-based access controls operate exactly as intended without technical failure.operated as intended in the tested scenarios.
5.3 Recommendations
In light of the findings and conclusions of this study, the researchers propose the following recommendations:
● System Improvement: The university may consider enhancing the system by adding payment anomaly detection to flag suspicious receipt submissions ahead of enrollment peaks. Additionally, refining OCR classification for low-quality scans could further improve document processing accuracy. Developers should also look into refining user interface elements related to Perceived Ease of Use, as it was the lowest-scoring construct at 4.62 despite its positive rating.
● Platform Expansion: To maximize reach and ensure inclusivity, future development should explore building a dedicated mobile application, since the system is currently accessed through mobile browsers. This would improve accessibility and offline resilience for students with limited connectivity on campus.
● Integration with Other University Systems: LSPU should explore integrating UniOrg database and reporting outputs with the registrar enrollment records and other campus information systems. This would facilitate seamless verification of student credentials and coordinated scheduling across academic units.
● Future Research: Future researchers are encouraged to evaluate the system using an extended TAM or UTAUT model that incorporates additional constructs such as trust and facilitating conditions. Furthermore, subsequent studies should investigate the long-term operational impact of automated accreditation and AI-assisted document processing on the actual cycle time of organization recognition at the campus.
